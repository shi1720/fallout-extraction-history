# Literature and contribution audit

Search and review date: 16 October 2026. This is a targeted scoping review, not a systematic review or proof that no earlier treatment exists.

## Candidate selection

A preliminary question concerned cross-site milk comparisons and calcium normalization. It was not selected: Soraya de Chadarevian's 2024 article directly discusses differences in diet and calcium across regions, as well as the construction of global fallout data. A paper claiming to discover that general issue would overstate novelty.

The selected question is narrower: why did selective extraction support a correction for collection resin while incomplete extraction prompted further tests and deferred correction for soil, within the same 1961 quarterly report? The underlying technical incidents were published by HASL; the historical contribution is their comparative reading, the distinction between the objects measured, and a reproducible numerical audit that preserves uncertainty about the reference assay.

Exact-title searches for the soil comparison found the primary report. Broader searches found the established histories above. No historical study of this specific paired comparison was located in the inspected material. Technical reports and non-digitized archival literature remain possible precedents.

## Claim review

- Defensible: the same report provides two different evidential roles for selective dissolution and different decisions about correction.
- Defensible: all sixteen printed rows support the reported descriptive calculations; they do not represent a global sample.
- Defensible: the table/summary minimum conflicts, and rounding of site 10 cannot reconcile it.
- Defensible: integer printing can reconcile an apparent SSE discrepancy; the witness does not recover the original raw observations.
- Not established: a new radiochemical method, a universal recovery factor, global fallout inventory changes, political motives for the deferred correction, a new health risk estimate, or universal historical priority.

## Further archival work

Private notebooks, resin supply records, correspondence, and the subsequent NBS blind-test results would extend the historical account. Their absence is an explicit boundary, not evidence that the planned tests failed or that a correction was politically suppressed. The present study is complete as a bounded reconstruction of the published decision, not as a complete institutional history of HASL.
